# Manufacturing Policy for India

> Status: working white-paper draft. This is the control document for the manufacturing paper. The short essay remains in [Industrial Base Math](./industrial-base-math.md); this file is where the research-grade argument is being built.

## Abstract

India should treat manufacturing as the main exception to a normally permission-light economic state. The argument is not nostalgia for factories or a return to the License Raj. It is that manufacturing creates learning externalities, supplier capabilities, export discipline, strategic options, and formal employment ladders that private firms cannot fully capture. A policy regime built around factory-time metrics, qualifying zero-tax treatment for new manufacturing profits, export discipline, and low-discretion state competition may fit India's state capacity better than discretionary winner-picking.

This paper develops that claim through five lenses: learning externalities, misallocation and the missing middle, premature deindustrialization, comparative industrialization cases, and Indian sectoral bottlenecks.

## Research Questions

Four questions govern the paper:

1. When does manufacturing deserve a policy wedge on welfare-theoretic grounds?
2. Which Indian manufacturing constraints are binding: tax, logistics, finance, labor thresholds, power, land, customs, supplier depth, or state capability?
3. Why is a broad, rule-based zero-tax regime superior to PLI-style fiscal transfers for most manufacturing formation?
4. What institutional design turns manufacturing rents into capability accumulation rather than capture?

The proposed state role is narrow: audit, registration, public measurement, and automatic eligibility rules, all easier to administer than discretionary winner-picking.

## 1. Executive Doctrine

The doctrine to be tested:

- small state for daily life;
- strong state for industrial capacity;
- free markets on national infrastructure;
- zero tax for qualifying new manufacturing capacity;
- factory-time as the operational measure of ease of doing business.

The burden of proof is high. A serious paper must show why manufacturing is special, why India is constrained, why broad low-discretion instruments are preferable to discretionary industrial policy, and how capture will be contained.

## 2. Contribution

The paper's intended contribution is a synthesis, not a claim of theoretical novelty. It connects:

- a learning-externality foundation from Greenwald-Stiglitz;
- a sector-choice apparatus from Hausmann-Hidalgo and Liu;
- an Indian constraint diagnosis from Hsieh-Klenow, Besley-Burgess, Kelkar-Shah, and Pritchett;
- a comparative discipline mechanism from Korea, Taiwan, Vietnam, Bangladesh, and China;
- a low-discretion policy design: zero tax for qualifying new manufacturing profits, audited domestic value addition, factory-time dashboards, export discipline, and automatic sunset.

The distinctive move is to argue that a right-wing, market-first Indian state can still make manufacturing the exception because the exception is rule-bound, measurable, and capacity-building. The operating principle is simple: stop taxing the formation of industrial capability, then force states to compete on the time it takes to build and run factories.

## 4. Formal Model

> Model status: sketch only. A full specification belongs in a separate technical note before any equation here is treated as the model.

The static fiscal intuition in the note is useful, but it is only a corner case. The model must make three choices explicit: the learning externality, the capability-stock law of motion, and the calibration strategy.

First, derive the policy wedge from learning externalities:

```text
private return = current-period producer surplus
social return = private return + learning spillovers + supplier capability + strategic option value
```

If the spillover term is positive and firms cannot appropriate it, the optimal wedge is positive. Under severe state-capacity constraints, `tau_c = 0` for qualifying profits can be treated as an administratively simple approximation to a more precise learning subsidy.

Second, use a two-stock capability model:

```text
K_t = physical capital stock
H_t = production capability stock
```

The provisional capability equation leaves `phi` unspecified:

```text
H_{t+1} = H_t + phi(Q_t, supplier_density_t, export_discipline_t) - delta_H H_t
```

The technical note will test a Cobb-Douglas learning function in production volume and supplier density, with an export-discipline multiplier and elasticities calibrated from ASI panels, sector studies, and comparative case evidence. The simulation set is baseline, PLI-only, broad zero tax, and broad zero tax plus narrow strategic-capacity premia; outputs are cumulative domestic value added, fiscal cost, formal payroll, exports, and capability stock after 10 and 20 years.

## 5. India in the Product Space

The product-space chapter has to start with numbers, not framework language: India's ECI rank and trajectory, Vietnam's movement, China's ascent, Bangladesh's concentration risk, and the density of India's unoccupied nearby products.

The policy consequence is sector discipline. Electronics assembly, pharma APIs, apparel, auto components, specialty steel, batteries, semiconductor ATMP, and wafer fabs are different policy problems. A right industrial policy for India separates adjacent capability bets from frontier strategic bets.

## 6. Misallocation and the Missing Middle

Anchor: Hsieh and Klenow's estimate that reducing Indian manufacturing misallocation to U.S.-like levels could raise Indian manufacturing TFP by roughly 40-60%.

The chapter connects that result to:

- size-dependent distortions;
- informality and threshold avoidance;
- finance constraints;
- state-level regulatory variation.

The 100,000-manufacturer target must not become a subsidy for smallness. It means many more firms capable of crossing scale thresholds, formalizing payroll, joining supplier networks, and exporting.

## 7. Premature Deindustrialization

Anchor: Rodrik's finding that developing countries are reaching peak industrialization earlier and at lower income levels than early industrializers. India's demographic window is also a deindustrialization window.

The opening table belongs before the theory: manufacturing employment share, manufacturing value-added share, year of peak, and per-capita income near peak for India, Korea, Taiwan, Malaysia, China, Vietnam, and Bangladesh. The core urgency claim is that India cannot wait for "basics first" to finish before manufacturing formation begins. The demographic window and manufacturing window are closing together.

## 8. Comparative Case Studies

The comparative chapter is jagged by design. Each case gets its own binding question:

- Korea HCI drive, 1973-1979: did capability persist after policy ended?
- Taiwan ITRI / TSMC: how did public research become a flagship firm?
- Vietnam FDI-led manufacturing: what does labor-cost arbitrage plus export discipline produce at scale?
- Bangladesh garments: when does labor-intensive success become a one-product trap?
- China Made in China 2025 / dual circulation: what does India face when the competitor is an adversary-scale state-firm bargain?

The point is comparative mechanism, not symmetry. Korea may need charts from Lane; Taiwan may need institutional narrative; Vietnam may need export and FDI data; Bangladesh may need quota history and concentration risk; China may need strategic-policy mapping.

## 9. Sectoral Institutional Chapters

The sector chapters also avoid a common mold. Each starts from its own industrial logic:

- Semiconductors: fab capex, node strategy, design depth, ATMP versus fab sequencing, and the Dholera/Tata-PSMC path after the Vedanta-Foxconn failure.
- Pharma APIs: molecule-level import dependence, bulk-drug-park execution, quality systems, and regulatory credibility.
- Electronics assembly: Foxconn Sriperumbudur, Tata Electronics, SMT-line depth, component ecosystems, testing, and supplier localization.
- Textiles and apparel: Tiruppur, Bangladesh competition, MMF transition, labor thresholds, and scale lock-in.
- Steel and auto: Bharat Forge, Tata/JLR, Maruti, CBAM exposure, EV transition, and supplier upgrading.

Every sector still needs named regulators, schemes, bottlenecks, and measurable targets, but the chapter shape follows the sector rather than a fixed checklist.

## 10. Political Economy and State Capability

This chapter is the guardrail against romantic industrial policy. Khan, Kelkar-Shah, Pritchett, Andrews, Woolcock, and state-level Indian evidence set the frame.

Core question:

What turns manufacturing rents into capability accumulation rather than rent dissipation?

Institutional design carries the answer: low-discretion eligibility, automatic sunset, audited domestic value addition, export discipline where relevant, public dashboards, independent audit, and revocation for non-performance.

## 11. Federal Architecture

Manufacturing is substantially state-mediated. Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh, and other relevant states need comparison on realized factory-time, industrial power, land, labor-code rollout, logistics, and supplier depth. The constitutional question is practical: what belongs to the Union, what belongs to states, and what needs inter-state coordination?

The likely architecture is a Manufacturing Council with GST-Council-style coordination but narrower scope: factory-time metrics, land-bank transparency, industrial power tariffs, labor-code rollout, logistics corridors, and common audit standards. The goal is race-to-the-top competition on time, reliability, and throughput rather than race-to-the-bottom fiscal concessions.

## 12. Strategic Capacity as Real Option

Broad zero-tax manufacturing policy is separate from narrow strategic-capacity premia for sectors where geopolitical option value is high: APIs, mature-node semiconductors, rare earths, drones, telecom/RAN, grid components, and batteries.

This chapter values domestic capacity under geopolitical volatility. Some sectors deserve a premium because the option value of domestic production rises sharply under supply shock, sanctions risk, war, pandemic, or export-control regimes, even when near-term employment is modest.

## 13. Objections

The paper must steelman:

- broad liberalization beats industrial policy;
- rent-seeking dominates learning;
- the Indian state lacks execution bandwidth;
- basics-first should precede industrial ambition.

The Kelkar-Shah/Pritchett objection gets the longest treatment. If India cannot run discretionary industrial policy well, the paper has to show why this proposal relies on lower-order state capabilities: registration, audit, tax administration, customs data, payroll records, and public dashboards.

## 14. Policy Package

Likely components:

- qualifying zero tax for new manufacturing profits;
- factory-time dashboard;
- export discipline for deeper benefits;
- domestic value-addition thresholds;
- sunset and audit architecture;
- strategic-capacity premia;
- Manufacturing Council for federal coordination.

## 15. Data and Replication Standard

Every numerical claim needs a trail. Minimum data work:

- DGCI&S bilateral trade by HS code;
- ASI industry/plant data where available;
- PLI approvals, disbursements, sales, exports, and employment by scheme;
- tariff schedules and input-output tables for effective protection;
- EPFO/ESI payroll data for wage-side fiscal capture;
- port dwell time, customs clearance, power connection, and land allotment data;
- state industrial-policy incentives and realized factory-time metrics.

Every table should identify source, year, unit, transformation, and uncertainty. Rounded estimates are better than fake precision.
